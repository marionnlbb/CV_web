2018 – High School

A first encounter with engineering

“This visit didn’t just teach me what engineering was, it showed me that I could be part of it.”

During my final year of high school, I had the chance to visit the Paris Air Show at Le Bourget with the association Elles Bougent. I wasn’t expecting much from a school trip… but it ended up changing far more than I imagined.

Throughout the day, I spoke with women engineers from a wide range of technical fields. Their stories, their passion, and the way they described their work opened a door I hadn’t really considered before. For the first time, I could genuinely picture myself in a technical career — one that blends rigor, creativity, and real-world impact.

That visit was a true turning point. I realized that engineering wasn’t just about technology or machines; it was a way to contribute meaningfully to society by designing, improving, and understanding complex systems. I left the salon with a clear sense that this world could become my own.

2021 – Choosing Science

“The scientific A-level was, for me, the first step toward engineering.”

Building on that first immersion into engineering, I decided to pursue a scientific track in high school. I selected Physics‑Chemistry, Mathematics, and Engineering Sciences as my main subjects, along with the Advanced Mathematics option, to strengthen the foundations I needed to continue in this direction.

from 2021 to 2025- Learning through service

“Serving in the reserve meant choosing to act rather than remain a spectator.”

In July 2021, alongside my academic studies, I joined the operational reserve of the French Air Force on the air base of Creil. I was driven by a strong desire to be useful, to serve, and to give concrete meaning to my commitment beyond the classroom.

During this three-year engagement, I held several roles combining operational, organizational and administrative responsibilities. I first contributed to the supervision of reservist training programs within CIRAAE and took part in military training courses focused on cohesion, discipline and operational readiness.

I also participated in Operation Sentinelle during the Olympic Games. Working in three-person teams, we were responsible for monitoring the aerial security of Paris. During the Games, no aircraft was allowed to fly over the city, as it was considered a protected airspace. Being part of this protection system placed me in a high-responsibility environment, requiring constant vigilance, coordination and strict adherence to security procedures.

I later transitioned to the Human Resources Office, specifically within the Chancellery Bureau. In this position, I managed administrative tasks requiring a high level of rigor and confidentiality. I was involved in the preparation of evaluation reports, the issuance of diplomas, and the management, classification and archiving of official documents.

On the air base, I contributed to the handling of more than 1000 military personnel files. This responsibility demanded strong organizational skills, precision and reliability, as well as the ability to work efficiently within structured and highly regulated systems.

This experience significantly strengthened my sense of responsibility, my attention to detail and my ability to operate in demanding environments. It also taught me how to evolve within large organizations, manage critical information and contribute effectively to missions with real operational impact.

2021 — Exploring to understand better

“Learning to build better.”

Autun — MPSI Preparatory Class

I began my higher education in a MPSI preparatory class in Autun, attracted by both scientific rigor and personal challenge. This demanding environment helped me develop discipline, resilience and a strong work ethic.

Beyond academics, I took part in military-style training periods and assumed leadership responsibilities as the female company representative, acting as an interface between students and the command structure.

This experience was extremely enriching, but it also made me realize that I needed a curriculum with more applied sciences and industrial perspectives.

Toulouse — L2 Mechanics (Université Paul Sabatier)

I then pursued a reinforced mechanics program at Paul Sabatier University, exploring engineering sciences through a university-based approach.

  • Electromagnetism
  • Optics
  • Particle mechanics
  • Solid mechanics
  • Fluid mechanics

This year confirmed my strong interest in physical modeling and in understanding how forces, flows and energy interact within complex systems.

Amiens — L3 Energy & Materials

Due to family circumstances, I completed my third year in Energy and Materials in Amiens. This year marked a decisive step by bringing together energy systems and materials science.

  • Energy conversion
  • Thermodynamics
  • Electrical engineering
  • Power electronics
  • Fluid mechanics
  • Materials science (metals, ceramics, polymers, composites)
  • Materials characterization

Through laboratory work, I particularly enjoyed testing and characterizing materials, strengthening my desire to pursue a path combining energy, materials and engineering.

2023- Choosing engineering

“Engineering as the clear choice, the school as the springboard.”

IMT Atlantique — Engineering Degree

After several academic paths, I chose to join an engineering school to gain a broader and more systemic understanding of complex technologies. IMT Atlantique stood out for its strong reputation in energy systems and its close connection with industry and international environments.

During my first year, the common core in mathematics and physics confirmed my strong interest in:

  • Thermodynamics
  • Fluid mechanics
  • Energy systems modeling

In my second year, I chose to specialize in nuclear energy, a demanding and often misunderstood field that I see as a key lever for future energy production.

This specialization allowed me to study:

  • Nuclear physics
  • Reactor operation and safety
  • Fuel cycle and waste management
  • Nuclear propulsion systems
  • Monte Carlo modeling
  • Human and organizational factors

What particularly resonated with me was the link between energy, materials and sustainability, especially through the study of irradiated materials, recycling and long-term environmental impact.

2025-A change of scenery, but not of mission.

“Continuing to serve, but this time in direct contact with the population.”

In 2025, after moving to Nantes, I could no longer continue my engagement within the Air Force reserve due to geographical constraints. However, my desire to remain committed and useful did not change.

Seeking a role closer to the population and everyday societal issues, I joined the operational reserve of the National Gendarmerie.

After completing the required military training (Block 1 and Block 2), I now serve as a brigadier-chief, reinforcing local units during specific and short-term missions.

I particularly appreciate night shifts, which I find more intense and formative, requiring adaptability, situational awareness and decision-making under pressure.

This commitment allows me to maintain a strong sense of service, responsibility and teamwork, while evolving in a context closely connected to the population and real-world situations.
